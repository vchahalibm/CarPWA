1. What map watchers do

Ordinary watchers look at a page or a feed on a schedule. Map watchers know where you are and where you're going: your phone's position while you drive, the route you're on (or a planned “from → to”), and your calendar's next place. They look ahead along the route and tell you, briefly and by voice, what matters before you get there: a jam forming, a faster way, an accident, the last fuel for a while, rain ahead, the next town, the time to leave.

  • Both places, as decided. The phone knows where you are; the Mac (StudioGen's agent browser) is what can read Google Maps. While you drive, DriveGen sends your position and route to your own StudioGen every half minute or so (through the mesh: a direct link, the relays, or StudioGBee; sealed; own devices only). The Mac runs the watchers, reads Google Maps, and sends the news back to the phone, which shows and speaks it.
  • Before you leave (leave-by, a planned trip), the Mac works alone from your calendar and your places (Settings › Location › Your place today; home and work places are added in phase 1).
  • No Mac reachable (away from home without a link): the phone falls back to open data for traffic-free basics (fuel stations and towns from Overpass, weather from Open-Meteo, the route from OSRM). Traffic, accidents and ratings need Google, so those wait until the Mac is back, and the phone says so once.
  • A Worker (always on) can run them too, so they work while the Mac sleeps.

4. Reading Google Maps

The agent browser opens Google Maps' own pages, as a person would, and reads them with the bridge (the same reading, Diagnose and self-healing as other watchers):

WhatPageWhat it reads
Directions with traffic and alternativesgoogle.com/maps/dir/?api=1&origin=LAT,LON&destination=…&travelmode=drivingEach route option: time in traffic, distance, “via” road, the “typical” time when shown, warnings (accident, closure, tolls)
Places along the waygoogle.com/maps/search/petrol+pump/@LAT,LON,14z at points a few km ahead on the routeName, rating and reviews, open now, distance; kept only when near the route ahead (and on your side, where it can tell)
A town or placegoogle.com/maps/place/… or a search for the townThe short description, rating, what it's known for

Map pages are visual and change often, so this relies on the debugging built for watchers: Diagnose (structure without words), Last check, the selector tester and practice copies. A practice Google Maps page (directions, alternatives, a places list with ratings) is part of phase 1, so the templates are tested before they meet the real site.

Google's terms don't allow automated reading of Google Maps; you chose it for its data (better in India), as with X. It reads gently (section 7). Google's paid Routes and Places APIs could replace page reading later without changing the watchers.

5. When they check: by distance and time

  • While driving: traffic and alternatives every 3–5 minutes (sooner near a junction where the alternative splits off); accidents with each traffic check; fuel and places every 10 km or so (sooner when fuel is low); weather every 15 minutes; next town when about 20 minutes away.
  • Only the road ahead counts: anything behind you or more than a few hundred metres off the route is ignored.
  • Not while parked: map watchers start when you start moving (DriveGen's GPS) and stop a few minutes after you stop.
  • Leave-by: from two hours before a calendar event with a place, every 15 minutes, then every 5 in the last half hour; quiet once you're moving towards it.

6. Telling you while you drive

  • Important (your choice: said aloud): a 5+ minute delay, a faster way, an accident or closure ahead, the last fuel for a long way, heavy rain ahead, leave-by and leave-now, running late for a meeting.
  • Nice to know (also said, your choice, but spaced out): the next town, fuel and food nearby, the weather turning. At most one every 10 minutes, never within a few hundred metres of a turn, never over a phone call or the navigation's own instructions; the rest waits as cards.
  • Short: one sentence, the number first (“12 minutes slower”, “6 km ahead”). Cards stay small while moving (DriveGen's driving rules); the full detail is there when you stop.
  • Acting asks first: taking the faster way (“Take it?” said and on the card) and telling someone you'll be late (a message, confirmed).

7. Privacy, manners and limits

  • Your position only goes between your own devices, sealed (the mesh's pairwise keys); StudioGBee never sees it. It isn't kept: the latest position and the route only, gone when you stop.
  • Google sees the searches the agent browser makes from your Mac (your route's places), as it would if you used Google Maps yourself.
  • Gentle on Google: one directions read every few minutes per drive, places only every 10 km, nothing while parked; a robot check pauses them and asks you, as for other watchers.
  • Limits: the phone needs data and the Mac (or a Worker) must be reachable for Google's data; StudioGBee keeps news while the phone is briefly out of coverage.

Lookahead (phase 2, built 3 November)

The traffic watcher became a lookahead watcher (template map.traffic, “Lookahead: traffic and what's on the way”; js/lookahead.js). It works in any city: nothing in it is tied to one place.

  • Where it looks from. Before you start: from the start you chose, and against your usual time for that trip at that hour on that weekday (from earlier days): “Today 55 min to Attibele: usually 35 at this time”. Once your phone's position is between the start and the end, from where you are, so only the road still ahead counts.
  • What it reads. Google Maps (times, alternatives, its own warnings, each route's turns and where they are). News: Google News (RSS, in your country and language, about the city and the roads and areas of the drive) and GDELT (world news). Posts: Reddit (its search feed) and X (in the agent browser, with your sign-in there; without it, X is skipped and Last check says so). Weather at the start, the middle and the end (Open-Meteo: heavy rain, thunderstorms, dense fog, strong wind, snow). Your own feeds (a city traffic police RSS, say).
  • What counts. Each item is sorted by the language model (prompt map.causes: kind, place, on the route yes/maybe/no, severity delay/caution/danger, until, one line), or by words without one. Kinds: accident, breakdown, heavy traffic, road works, closure, diversion, VIP movement, protest, strike, procession, event crowd, flooding, fallen tree, landslide, fire, signals down, police checks, weather, other. It counts when it names a road or area of the drive, or when its place, found on the map, lies within about 1 km of a route's path (from its turns). Each is told once.
  • What it says. “Protest: Hosur Road near Bommanahalli, on your way to Attibele”, the line and its source, and the other way: the fastest route without it (up to 15 minutes longer when it avoids something, else only when faster): “Or via Sarjapur Rd: 60 min · 14 min longer · avoids it”. Also when traffic adds 5+ minutes, when it eases, and Google's own warnings, each with the other way when there is one.
  • The map in the card. Your route (grey), the other way (blue), what was found (orange), drawn on OpenStreetMap's tiles: each route follows the roads through its own turns (OSRM with stops). A picture of Google's own map isn't possible: the agent browser isn't drawn while it's hidden, and StudioGen keeps watching with its window closed.
  • Take it. On the card, or by saying yes when the phone asks “Take Sarjapur Rd?”: DriveGen re-routes through the other way while it navigates (OSRM, a stop on that road), else Google Maps navigates through it (comgooglemaps:// on phones).
  • Cost. The first check of a drive reads each route's turns and places them on the map (about a minute); they're kept 12 hours. The sources are read at most every 10 minutes, X every 15, GDELT once per 5 seconds at most.

Along the way (phase 3, built 3 November)

  • Fuel or charging on the way (map.fuel): Google Maps searches (“petrol pump”, “gas station” in the US and Canada, “CNG station”, “EV charging station”) at points 15, 30, 45 and 60 km ahead; places within 1 km of the route and ahead of you, open (or opening), at least the rating you set. Told: the last one before a gap longer than you set (30 km), none in 60 km, or the nearest when you ask for it. The bridge's links reads each place's position from its address (!3d!4d).
  • Weather ahead (map.weather): Open-Meteo at points every 10 km ahead, at the hour you'll reach each; the first thunderstorm, heavy rain, rain, snow, fog or strong wind, with the town there.
  • Next town (map.town): the town about 12–20 minutes ahead (OpenStreetMap), when it isn't the one you're in: Wikipedia's first sentence (in your language when it has the article), the weather there now, a local headline (Google News).
  • Speaking while driving (RoadVoice, on the phone): important news when the voice is free, never within 300 m of a turn, during a call or while a question waits; nice-to-know at most every 4 minutes, dropped after 10; “Take X? Say yes or no” for the other way.
  • The phone with no Mac (RoadLocal, Settings › Location): while DriveGen navigates and no Mac of yours can be reached, every 5 minutes along its own route: the weather ahead, the next town, and fuel from OpenStreetMap (Overpass, within 800 m of the route ahead).
  • Turns read by one map watcher are kept for every map watcher to the same place (12 hours); fuel, weather and town read only the route you'll drive.

8. Later watchers

Food at meal times (ratings, near where you'll be at lunch) · tolls and toll costs ahead · speed cameras · parking near the destination · “10 minutes away” (tell someone) · the weekday commute (usual 35 minutes, today 55) · tomorrow's long drive, the evening before (weather, closures, events on the route) · a place opening or getting less busy · EV charger availability · air quality ahead · sunset during the drive · border or state crossings (permits, tax).
