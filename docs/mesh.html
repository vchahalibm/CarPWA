2. The views in detail

Live (the graph)

Every device as a node (app, device type, person, role), every link as a line (direct / relay / Tailscale, latency, traffic), what each is doing right now (script and step, job and progress, idle), and flowing dots for live requests. Click a node or a line for its details; drag to arrange; layouts: force-directed, by person, by role, by location (when devices share it).

Devices

A table: name, app and version, device and OS, person, role, online since / last seen, network type (from Test the connection), link to you, relays used, secret age and phrase, capabilities (scripts offered, tasks, data sources, screens), permissions; actions: rename, permissions, change secret, revoke, ping, test the connection remotely.

Activity

A live, searchable feed of everything that happened: joins and leaves, requests (who asked whom, what, result, time, size), scripts started and steps, jobs, link changes (direct ↔ relay, broker failovers), shares, errors. Filter by device, person, kind; click to see the device or the show.

Shows (mesh mode)

Synchronised multi-screen shows as swim lanes: one lane per screen, current step, readiness, drift against the conductor, failures; controls for the conductor (start, next, back, pause, drop a screen).

Shared

The shared library across the mesh: scripts, motions, recordings, data; who shared it, version, size, which devices have a copy, which are private (only on their own StudioGen).

History and analysis

Over days and weeks: who was online when, link quality (share of direct vs relay, latency trends, failovers), busiest scripts and devices, failures and their causes, relay health; charts with the data widget's engine; export as CSV/JSON; “what happened at 10:28?” replay of the graph.

3. Where the information comes from

Every app already knows its own part (its links, its relays, what it runs). The mesh view needs each app to report that, small and encrypted, and StudioGen to collect it.

Event (sent by each device)CarriesWhen
presencedevice id, name, app + version, device/OS, person, role, network type, battery/sleep state (optional)on start, every 30 s (with the hello it already sends)
linksfor each peer: kind (direct / relay / tailscale), relays in use, RTT, bytes, sinceon change, and every 60 s
capabilitiesscripts offered, task kinds, data sources, screens, artifacts shared (ids, versions)on change
activityrequest start/end (from, to, kind, script name, result, duration, size), script start/step/end, job progress, errorsas it happens
syncshow id, step, ready/done/failed, clock offset and drifteach step, and every 2 s during a show

Events go on the mesh's group topic (encrypted with the group key, signed by the device), so every StudioGen in the mesh can show the view; DriveGens publish but don't need to keep anything. StudioGen keeps the history in its own store (a local database in its data folder), with a retention you choose.

4. Architecture

 every app ── Telemetry (new)  ──►  group topic of the mesh (relays; or direct links)  ──►  StudioGen(s)
   ├ presence / links / capabilities / activity / sync events               │                 ├ Mesh store: devices, links, events (history)
   ├ signed with the device key (identity)                                   │                 ├ Graph model: nodes + edges, live state
   └ encrypted with the group key                                            │                 ├ Mesh screen: Live · Devices · Activity · Shows · Shared · History
                                                                            ▼                 └ Analysis: charts (data widget), export
                                    Roster (signed by owners): who is in the mesh, roles, permissions
  • Identity: each app makes a device key pair once (ECDSA P-256 for signing, ECDH P-256 for pairwise keys, both in WebCrypto); its id is a hash of the public key. Every event is signed, so nobody in the mesh can pretend to be another device.
  • Roster: the member list (device id, keys, name, person, role, who invited it, permissions), versioned and signed by an owner; any member with an invite can add a device (as decided), owners can remove.
  • Group key: encrypts the group topic; rotated when someone is removed (owner-signed update), plus the pairwise keys we already have for one-to-one requests.
  • Collectors: every StudioGen keeps the mesh store; one is “primary” for the history (the one you open most), the others can fill gaps.
  • Graph: the edges come from both ends' links reports (each link is confirmed by both sides; a one-sided report shows as “trying”).
  • Scale: dozens of devices comfortably (events are small, the relays already carry the hellos); a few hundred with sampling.

5. Privacy and control

  • Telemetry carries names and numbers (which script, how long, how many rows, which link), never the contents (no data rows, no spoken text, no file contents).
  • Private artifacts and scripts are never announced. Each device can choose how much it reports (full / presence only / nothing beyond requests it makes).
  • Only mesh members can read the group topic; events are signed; the history stays on StudioGen and can be wiped or exported.

6. Phases (proposal)

  1. V1 · Live view: device identities, the mesh (create, invite, roster), presence + links + capabilities telemetry, StudioGen's Mesh screen with the Live graph and Devices table; the devices you've already paired join automatically.
  2. V2 · Activity: activity events (requests, scripts, jobs, link changes), the Activity feed, moving dots on the graph; request mode by device and capability (“run Export on the Office Mac”).
  3. V3 · Shows: mesh mode (the conductor, steps and tandem, shared clock) and the Shows swim lanes.
  4. V4 · Shared: the artifact catalogue, fetch and cache, the private flag, the Shared view.
  5. V5 · History and analysis: retention, charts, export, replay; owner-signed updates and group-key rotation on removal.

7. Questions for you

  1. Where should the Mesh view live in StudioGen: a new top-level screen (and menu-bar item), a separate window you can put on a second display, or inside Settings › Connections?
  2. Should DriveGen show anything of the mesh (a small “who's around / what's running” card), or nothing?
  3. What may devices report: names and numbers only (proposed), or also contents for debugging (e.g. the first lines of a result)?
  4. How long should StudioGen keep the history: a day, a week, a month, or until you clear it?
  5. The first build: V1 + V2 (live graph, devices, activity) as proposed, or something else first?

8. Decided and built

  • Where: a new top-level Mesh screen in StudioGen (app list, menu-bar item “Mesh…”, the Mesh widget). Files: js/mesh.js (the mesh: identities, roster, invites, rooms, telemetry, journal, shows, sharing, requests, history store) and js/meshview.js (the screen).
  • DriveGen: a small who's-around card (its Mesh screen and the top of the Your-Mac sheet): devices, what they're doing, the scripts you may run on them.
  • Contents: short contents for debugging (cut to 80 characters over relays), never secrets.
  • History: kept a week by default; 1, 7, 30 or 90 days in Mesh › History (IndexedDB dd-mesh on StudioGen).
  • Not overloading the relays: what a device reports depends on the path. Over a direct link: everything as it happens. Over relays: presence in one message every 15 seconds, activity batched every 30 seconds, at most 2 KB a minute per device, contents cut short, show progress only when the step changes. Every device keeps its own journal; StudioGen fetches the details only when you open a device (“Fetch details”).
  • Security: each device has its own signing key (ECDSA P-256) and a key pair for pairwise keys with each member (ECDH); the owner signs the roster; invites are signed by the member who made them; removing a member changes the group key, sent to the others through their own pairwise rooms.
  • Tested: three devices in the lab (two StudioGens, one DriveGen): joining by invite and by pairing, a request from DriveGen run on the second StudioGen, sharing a script, a show in steps (drift 2–3 ms), removal.
